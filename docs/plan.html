Lift tier Built

Binary in, pseudo-Pascal out

A p-code decoder passing a strict self-check on all 287 procedures, a stack-merging lifter, and a structuriser. Calibrated three ways against real source: the two GOTOXY programs whose .TEXT and .CODE both sit on the same disk; 41 operating-system procedures against the UCSD II.0 source; and PRINTERROR's nested case, where 31 error strings come out character for character.

Still unchecked against source: record field offsets and with, and sets.

Fast tier Built

Source in, codefile out — in a second

Peter Miller's ucsdpsys_compile, built reproducibly under WSL and driven from Windows. On both reference programs it reproduces Apple's p-code byte for byte, apart from one alignment byte per procedure that Apple writes as 0 and it writes as NOP.

It earned its place immediately — it compiled the declaration skeleton on the first run and found a two-word error in the global frame that thirty findings of reading the binary had missed. But it is more permissive than Apple's compiler, so "it compiles" here is the weaker claim. Where it disagrees with the binary, the binary wins.

Working rules the ones that were learned the hard way

Label every claim

Verified binary fact, verified source fact, strong inference, or speculation. The inherited work's biggest failure was a mislabelled inference promoted to a headline correction.

Prefer a check the binary can fail

And check that it can fail for the property you care about. A linear sweep re-synchronises after corruption and still lands on the right end address, so 287/287 said nothing about seven bad bytes. A branch-target check found them at once.

A total that does not balance is evidence

The global map printed "1225 of 1222" for thirty findings and it was read as slack every time. It was a two-word error. Require the sum to come out exactly, or say why it cannot.

Ask whether the artifact already carries the name

The codefile's SEGNAME field holds each segment procedure's own identifier. One name invented two findings earlier turned out to be printed in the binary all along.

Get a second opinion, then a third

Our reader agreeing with our writer proves nothing. An independent filesystem implementation found a defect the self-checks could not see; Apple's own compiler then found two more.

Nothing in evidence is ever modified

Everything under build/, analysis/ and reference_source/ is generated. Never hand-edit it; change the tool and rerun.

Rename late rather than twice

A recovered name can be wrong, and it shows up as a second routine behaving more like the name than the one holding it. Write the evidence down and leave the name alone until the answer is known.

The binary wins

Over the UCSD II.0 source, over Tribby's 1.2 listing, over Parker's notes, over the fast tier's output. Every one of them has been wrong about Apple at least once.
